Maximizing Foot Traffic in Genesis Cities

Published by Raytown Construction | Data Provided by FASTTRACK TOOLs

Developing a high-yield neighborhood in Upland requires more than just minting properties and dropping standard models. To truly maximize a node's potential, we must look at the underlying traffic patterns and structural density.

The FASTTRACK Assessment

Our recent scan of prime Genesis City nodes using FASTTRACK TOOLs revealed a significant inefficiency in how most players utilize their Sparklet. Over 60% of commercial zones are placed in low-traffic corridors, severely limiting their long-term value.

Key FastTrack Metrics (Sample Data)

  • Optimal Commercial Density: 3-4 storefronts per high-traffic transit block.
  • Residential Support: Every commercial structure requires a baseline of 5 adjacent residential properties to stabilize neighborhood value.
  • Sparklet Waste: Building factories in designated residential nodes increases construction time by an average of 14% due to poor zoning alignment.

Raytown's Strategic Implementation

Based on these insights, Raytown Construction recommends a tiered development approach. By clustering high-tier residential builds near transit hubs and creating a perimeter of commercial structures, we create a self-sustaining ecosystem of foot traffic.
